Where it comes from

My dad played college football and has spent his career since as a 4th grade teacher. I've watched him treat every kid in his classroom like they're his own, and that's more or less how I've come to think about finance: Anyone can go through the motions on data analysis, but it takes genuine care for the companies and the data itself to add real value. 

My mom was an executive editor at People, which meant she spent my childhood on flights, running teams and making decisions I did not really  understand at the time. What I did understand, watching her, was how deft she was at managing people under real time pressure, a skill that maps onto markets more directly than I expected.

I also have a twin brother, and we've been working as a team since before we could really call it that: same room, same teams, same tests, learning early how to split up work and actually rely on someone else to hold up their end of it.

I also have two younger sisters, ten and thirteen years behind me. With that kind of gap, I'm either a cautionary tale or an example, there's no in-between, and I've come to see that pressure as a privilege.
